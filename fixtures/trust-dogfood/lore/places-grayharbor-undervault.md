# Place Notes — Grayharbor and the Undervault

## Grayharbor

Grayharbor is a port city of six terraces cut into black basalt cliffs above
a deep-water harbor. The terraces are linked by stair-streets and rope
lifts; the lowest terrace floods at spring tide and everyone pretends this
is fine. Principal institutions: the Terrace Council (civic government),
the Grand Weighing House on the third terrace (all cargo and salvage is
weighed, taxed, and brokered there), the Cinder Compact hall, and the
Surveyors' Guild. The fish market and Netmaker's Row occupy the second
terrace. Lamplighters maintain the terrace lamps on a strict dusk circuit;
the lamp-house is their guild hall.

Notable residents referenced in the manuscript: Odessa Vane-Kir, the
Weighing House's senior salvage broker, called Dess by those she permits;
her office sits behind an unmarked door at the back of the Weighing House,
and her family has brokered Vault salvage since before the Compact existed.

## The Undervault

The Undervault — delvers simply say "the Vault" — is a sealed pre-Collapse
complex beneath the harbor cliffs, entered through the Salt Door: a carved
stone slab at the cliff base, submerged except at low tide. The spring
quake cracked the Door's seal after eleven centuries; the Cinder Compact
claimed delving rights within the week.

Known interior, per Compact surveys: an antechamber with twelve pillars, a
floor channel, a dais bearing a dormant warding circle, and an inner gate
of dark metal locked by an emberglass socket. Below the gate, a gallery
patrolled by husks — the Vault's dead-that-tally, animate remains in
corroded old-world armor — and beyond it, an underground city whose
avenues all channel toward a central well ringed by twelve seated metal
figures.

Hazards: warding circles (dormant, violently dischargeable); husks
(dangerous in close quarters, vulnerable to sorrowsteel and aether
discharge); and Vaultburn — a creeping gray miasma that infects the living,
suppresses aether recovery, and marks the skin with pewter threads. Wardlight
lanterns burning aether-oil keep the miasma at bay. Established treatment:
salt, cedar oil, and whitethorn ash over roughly a week, a Vane-Kir family
remedy predating the Compact.

The Emberglass Key: a palm-sized faceted shard of orange glass, warm to the
touch, recovered from the antechamber channel after the ward discharge. It
is the only known means of opening the inner gate.
