# Chapter Four — Sorrowsteel

The Warden-Council took nine days to debate the gate report, which in Compact terms was reckless haste. The ruling came down on a rain-gray morning: the inner Vault would be surveyed, one team, senior warden supervising, junior delver mapping. Everyone on the third terrace understood what wasn't written — the Council wanted to know what was down there before the man with the salt-white hair found his own way in.

Odessa sent a runner before they went. The note said only: *Come by the back. Bring the girl.*

"I don't lend," she said, when they stood in her corner office again. "Understand that first. This is a loan against my own better judgment, which makes it the most expensive kind."

She laid it on the desk in a sheath of oiled black leather: a knife long as Sera's forearm, dark-bladed, the metal holding a sheen like deep water under moonlight. The grip was wrapped in wire gone smooth with age.

"Sorrowsteel," Odessa said. "Old-world make — the only metal that ever held an edge against husk plate. My grandmother took this one out of the Vault under the Narrows the year the Compact was founded. It has opened four generations of things that should have stayed shut." She pushed it an inch toward Sera. "Your little discharge trick emptied half your aether on one husk. The gallery below the gate won't send you one."

Sera took the knife. It was lighter than it looked and it settled into her grip like it had opinions about being put down. She belted the sheath at her left hip, drew, checked the balance, sheathed it again.

"Fee?" she asked.

"Bring it back," said Odessa Vane-Kir, "and bring yourself with it. Dess doesn't stock replacements for either."

---

Corvo Lash was waiting at the head of the cliff stair again. This time there were wardens present, two of them, and he stood between them chatting as though they were his own doormen. The Compact's wardens, Sera noted, were both very carefully looking at the sea.

"Down again, Ledgerbound?" His voice was all pleasant weather. "And armed for it. Look at that blade. My principals would pay the price of a terrace house just to weigh that knife."

"Your principals," Sera said, "can apply at the Weighing House like everyone else."

"Oh, we've applied." Something flickered under the pleasantness, quick as a fin breaking water. "The broker keeps refusing our custom. It grieves us. So many things in Grayharbor for sale, and its people insist on the few that aren't." He stepped aside from the stairhead with a small bow. "Mind the tide, Sera Kestrel. It forgets no one."

Going down the black stair with his eyes on her back was worse than the ward-scar, worse than the husk. Don't look up, she told herself, and made her boots take the worn bowls of the steps one at a time until the cliff hid her.

"He knew we'd come today," Brannic said quietly, at the bottom. "Council only ruled this morning."

"Then the leak isn't a floor of the Compact hall, Bran. It's sitting at the table."

Brannic said nothing at all, which was its own answer.

---

The Salt Door. The antechamber. The dark gate — signed out of Odessa's deep vault that morning, the Key had turned the lock like a word in the right ear, and gone back into Brannic's breast pocket after, against its sworn return to the Weighing House by evening bell. Down the narrow stair, into the cold that owned the underworld, and this time past the gallery, deeper than any Compact map had ever run.

The Vault below the gallery was a city. That was the only honest word Sera's pen could find for it. Streets of pale stone, dry as bone under a ceiling lost in dark; doorways in ranks; the shallow channels running down every avenue toward some deeper center, all of it silent, all of it waiting, the gray miasma pooled in the cross-streets like fog in a harbor with no wind to move it.

They kept to the wardlight and the widest avenue. Sera mapped in fast strokes — bearings, paces, the tall shapes of what might have been counting-houses for gods. Twice the haze bulged toward them from a side street and twice the lantern's blue-white pushed it back.

The third time, it didn't bulge. It stood up.

Two husks this time, unfolding from a doorway in that terrible last-inch way, plate scraping on stone. Sera's aether was a quarter-pool at best; a discharge would empty her against one of them and leave the second unpaid. Instead her hand went to the left hip like it had trained there for years.

Sorrowsteel went through corroded plate as if the plate were an argument the blade had already won. The first husk folded around the cut, dust sighing from its seams. The second clipped her shoulder and drove her into the wall — the same shoulder, always the same shoulder — before she got the dark blade up and put it through the thing's helm, and the Vault's silence came back down like snow.

"Anything past a bruise?" Brannic's lantern was there, and his hand.

"The wall and I have an understanding by now." She flexed her fingers around the wire grip. "Bran. This knife."

"Aye. Now you know why the Court wants to weigh it." He said the name deliberately, watching the empty avenue. "And why Dess wouldn't sell."

They surveyed to the tide's last safe hour. At the far end of the great avenue, where the channels all ran, the map's lines converged on something Sera drew from four hundred paces and would not walk closer to describe: a well of pale stone wide as the Weighing House, ringed by twelve figures of dark metal, seated, still, their heads bowed — and around the well's rim, faint as a banked fire, a light the exact color of emberglass, breathing.

Rising, very slightly, with each breath.

"Done," Brannic said softly, looking at it. "We're done. Up. Now."

Neither of them spoke again until the Salt Door was locked at their backs and the sea was hauling itself home over the shelf below. On the black stair, in the wind, Brannic finally stopped, two steps above her, and looked down with an expression she had never seen him wear.

"Whatever wakes down there," he said, "the Key is the only word it listens for. Remember where it sleeps and who holds the tag. If anything happens to me, that arrangement is the only will I've got."

"Bran—"

"Climb," said Warden Halloway, and climbed.
