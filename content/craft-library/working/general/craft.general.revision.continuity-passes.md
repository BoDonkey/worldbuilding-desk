## What it is

A continuity pass is a dedicated revision read-through with a single,
narrow focus: consistency of names, dates, physical descriptions,
established facts, and timeline, rather than structure, prose quality, or
overall story assessment. It's a distinct pass from the developmental and
line-level revision covered in this library's triage record, because the
attention required is different in kind — a continuity pass asks "does this
match what was established earlier," not "does this work" or "is this well
written," and trying to hold both kinds of attention simultaneously tends
to mean neither gets done thoroughly.

## Why readers may care

Continuity errors — a character's eye color changing, a stated travel time
that doesn't match a later mentioned distance, a secondary character's name
shifting — don't usually break a story's structure, but they erode a
reader's trust in the manuscript's care and can pull a reader out of the
fictional experience at moments that have nothing to do with the story's
actual content. Because these errors are often invisible to an author deep
in developmental or line-level thinking (attention is elsewhere), they tend
to require their own dedicated pass to catch reliably.

## Common forms and variants

- **Fact-tracking passes**, focused on concrete, checkable details (names,
  dates, physical descriptions, established rules) using an external
  reference document (a "bible") built alongside the manuscript.
- **Timeline passes**, focused specifically on chronology — travel times,
  seasons, the sequence and spacing of events — which can be harder to
  track than static facts because they require holding a whole sequence in
  mind at once.
- **Established-rule passes**, specific to genre fiction with explicit
  systems (magic rules, world logic), checking that stated limits and
  mechanics are honored consistently — closely related to several
  system-mechanic patterns' own consistency concerns.
- **Reader-facing versus author-only continuity**, distinguishing errors a
  reader would actually notice (a named character's described appearance
  changing) from ones that are technically inconsistent but unlikely to
  register (a very minor, unstated detail).

## What it can look like in an author's process

- A separate document or tracking system (character sheet, timeline,
  system-rules reference) maintained alongside drafting and checked during
  a dedicated pass.
- A revision pass conducted with no attention to prose quality or
  structure, purely cross-referencing details against earlier
  established facts.
- The inverse, as a warning sign: continuity checking folded into a
  general revision pass with no dedicated attention, or no external
  reference document at all for a manuscript with many trackable details.

## Common failure modes

- **No dedicated pass at all**, relying on memory or general revision
  attention to catch continuity errors, which tends to miss more than a
  focused pass would.
- **Continuity tracking abandoned partway through a long project**, common
  in extended series or serialized fiction, where an initially maintained
  reference document falls behind the manuscript's actual state.
- **Over-scrupulous continuity checking that chases invisible errors**,
  spending disproportionate revision time on inconsistencies no reader
  would plausibly notice, at the expense of higher-priority developmental
  or line-level work.

## Questions for the author

- Is there a dedicated pass, separate from developmental and line-level
  revision, focused only on consistency?
- Is a reference document (character details, timeline, established rules)
  being maintained and kept current as the manuscript grows or changes?
- For a long or serialized project, has continuity tracking kept pace with
  the manuscript, or has it fallen behind?
- Is continuity-checking effort proportional to what a reader would
  actually notice, or is it consuming revision time disproportionate to
  its impact?

## Revision or design experiments

1. Before a continuity pass, build or update a reference document listing
   key names, dates, physical details, and established rules.
2. Conduct a pass focused exclusively on cross-referencing the manuscript
   against that document, with no attention to prose quality.
3. For a long or serialized project, schedule periodic continuity check-ins
   rather than relying on a single pass at the end.
4. Triage found inconsistencies by reader-visibility before spending
   revision time on the least noticeable ones.

## When this advice does not apply

- Very short manuscripts with few trackable details may not need a
  formalized continuity pass or reference document.
- Some genres or styles with intentionally loose, dreamlike continuity
  (certain literary or surrealist traditions) may not prioritize strict
  consistency as a value at all.

## Evidence and detection limits

This is a practice pattern concerning an author's revision process; whether
a dedicated continuity pass occurred isn't observable from the finished
manuscript alone, though its *absence* often becomes visible through
inconsistencies a reader can point to directly. Where a project maintains
explicit structured data about names, dates, or established facts linked to
scenes, cross-referencing that data against the manuscript text could
identify candidate inconsistencies more directly than an unstructured
continuity pass could, though confirming an inconsistency's severity and
reader-visibility remains an interpretive judgment.

## Original micro-examples

Not applicable in the usual sense — this pattern concerns process rather
than manuscript content directly, though its failure is often visible in
the manuscript itself. An author-facing illustration: a serialized story
establishes a secondary character's hometown in chapter four; forty
chapters later, without a continuity pass or reference document, the same
character is described as being from a different town, with no narrative
explanation — an error a dedicated pass, cross-referenced against a
maintained character sheet, would likely have caught before publication.
