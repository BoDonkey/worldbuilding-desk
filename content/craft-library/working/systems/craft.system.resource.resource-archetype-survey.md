## What it is

Beyond mana and qi (covered in their own comparison record), several other
resource archetypes recur across progression fiction, each arriving with a
default narrative texture readers will half-expect even before an author
states anything about it. Naming that default texture explicitly — and
deciding whether to honor or deliberately subvert it — is more useful than
treating every resource as an interchangeable fuel bar with a different
label.

## Why readers may care

A resource's default texture shapes reader expectation before a single rule
is stated: "stamina" implies physical limitation and exhaustion; "rage"
implies emotional volatility and a cost to control; "faith" implies a
relationship with something outside the self, with obligations attached.
When a system's actual behavior matches its resource's default texture,
readers absorb the logic quickly; when it doesn't, the mismatch needs to be
addressed directly or it reads as an unexamined label rather than a
worldbuilding choice.

## Common forms and variants

- **Stamina**, physical endurance, generally renewable through rest,
  implying limits tied to the body rather than the supernatural — common in
  systems wanting a grounded, physically legible cost.
- **Rage or fury**, generated by combat or provocation rather than rested
  into existence, implying a resource that's earned through emotional or
  physical intensity and often difficult to fully control.
- **Faith or divine favor**, implying an external relationship — power
  granted rather than possessed, often with obligations, rituals, or moral
  conditions attached to continued access.
- **Psionics**, implying a resource rooted in the mind, often associated
  with mental strain, sanity cost, or vulnerability to psychic
  counterattack as its characteristic risk.
- **Soul or spirit**, implying the resource is constitutive of selfhood,
  raising stakes around identity, corruption, or loss of self when spent
  carelessly.
- **Blood**, implying a visceral, embodied, often morally weighted cost —
  frequently tied to sacrifice, kinship, or literal life.
- **Life force**, the most direct resource-as-mortality framing, where
  expenditure literally shortens a character's lifespan — among the highest-
  stakes resource choices available.
- **Hybrid resources**, combining two archetypes' textures deliberately
  (blood-faith, rage-soul) to create a resource whose cost and texture are
  genuinely distinct from either parent archetype alone.

## What it can look like on the page

- A resource's expenditure narrated in a way consistent with its
  archetype's default texture — stamina depletion shown through physical
  exhaustion, faith expenditure shown through ritual obligation or divine
  attention.
- A deliberate subversion, clearly signaled, where a resource behaves
  against its default texture for a specific narrative reason (a "faith"
  resource that requires no belief at all, revealed as a twist about the
  deity's true nature).
- The inverse, as a warning sign: a resource named "rage" that behaves
  identically to a calm, rested mana pool, with no emotional texture
  attached to its generation or expenditure at all.

## Progression, failure behavior, and cross-mechanic interaction

Different archetypes imply different failure behaviors worth deciding
deliberately: running out of stamina implies collapse or vulnerability;
running out of faith might imply abandonment by a patron deity, with social
and narrative consequences beyond the mechanical; running out of soul or
life force implies a much higher-stakes failure state, potentially
including permanent character change or death. These archetypes also
interact with each other and with class or role systems — a rage-based
resource pairs naturally with a berserker-adjacent role, while a faith
resource implies an institutional or relational structure (a priesthood, a
patron) that the crafting-and-economy and systemic-social-consequences
records both touch on from other angles.

## Common failure modes

- **A named resource with no matching texture**, where "rage" or "faith" is
  used as a label with no accompanying behavioral or narrative logic
  distinguishing it from a generic mana-equivalent.
- **Inconsistent emotional or relational logic**, where a resource's
  narrative texture (faith requiring devotion, rage requiring provocation)
  is honored in some scenes and ignored in others.
- **Underexplored high-stakes archetypes**, where a resource like soul or
  life force is used mechanically (as a spendable pool) without the story
  engaging the identity or mortality stakes the archetype implies.

## Questions for the author

- Does this resource's expenditure and recovery behave consistently with
  its named archetype's default texture?
- If the resource is soul-, blood-, or life-force-based, does the story
  engage the identity or mortality stakes that archetype implies, or does
  it function as a reskinned mana pool?
- Is there a scene where the resource's generation (rage through
  provocation, faith through ritual) is dramatized, rather than the
  resource simply being available?

## Revision or design experiments

1. Name the resource's archetype explicitly and write one sentence
   describing its default narrative texture; check the manuscript's actual
   use of the resource against that sentence.
2. For a high-stakes archetype (soul, life force), find or add a scene
   where the identity or mortality cost is directly engaged, not just
   mechanically tracked.
3. For a relationally-implied resource (faith), find a scene showing the
   relationship (a ritual, an obligation, a moment of doubt) rather than
   only the resource's numeric availability.

## When this advice does not apply

- A story deliberately using a resource name ironically or as a
  deconstruction (a "faith" resource in a setting with no actual gods,
  revealed as self-belief) is using the mismatch on purpose, provided it's
  eventually addressed.
- Very lightweight systems where the resource is purely decorative and
  never load-bearing for plot may not need this level of textural
  consistency.

## Evidence and detection limits

Whether a resource's use is consistent with its named archetype's default
texture requires reading and interpreting the manuscript's worldbuilding
and prose — a judgment no structured data substitutes for.

## Original micro-examples

*Textureless naming:* A character's "rage meter" fills steadily during
combat and empties smoothly when spent on an ability, functioning
identically to a calm, rested mana pool with a different label and no
emotional volatility.

*Textured resource:* The same rage resource instead fills unpredictably,
faster when the character is provoked or wounded, and using it at full
capacity risks a loss of tactical control the story dramatizes directly —
the archetype's implied emotional cost is mechanically and narratively
present.
