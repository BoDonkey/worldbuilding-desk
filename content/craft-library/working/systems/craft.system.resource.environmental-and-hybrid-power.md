## What it is

Environmental power is a resource whose availability depends on a specific
place or condition — a mage who draws strength from a particular ley
line, a cultivator whose power intensifies under moonlight, a character
tied to a specific terrain or season — making location and circumstance
part of the resource's own logic rather than an incidental setting detail.
A hybrid resource deliberately combines two archetypes' textures (a
blood-faith resource, a rage-soul resource) to create something whose cost
and behavior are genuinely distinct from either parent archetype alone,
rather than simply being a mana-equivalent with two labels attached. Both
raise a design question the more common, personally-carried resource
models (mana, qi, stamina) don't need to answer as directly: what happens
to a character's power when the environment shifts, or when a hybrid's two
component conditions pull in different directions.

## Why readers may care

Environmental power ties a character's capability to the wider world in a
way personal resources don't, which can generate distinctive plot and
travel stakes — a character weakened far from their power source, or
strategically positioning themselves near one, makes geography narratively
load-bearing rather than incidental. Hybrid resources, done well, offer a
texture no single archetype can — a resource that requires both faith and
sacrifice, for instance, implies a character whose power depends on
maintaining two different kinds of commitment simultaneously, which can
generate tension when those commitments conflict.

## Common forms and variants

- **Location-bound power**, strongest or only available at a specific
  place, making travel and geography directly relevant to a character's
  capability.
- **Condition-bound power**, tied to a recurring but not fixed condition
  (weather, time of day, lunar cycle, proximity to a specific kind of
  event) rather than a single location.
- **Ambient-field power**, drawn from a broader regional or global
  condition (a magical field that varies in strength across a setting)
  rather than a single discrete source.
- **Additive hybrid**, combining two archetypes' costs and behaviors
  straightforwardly (both faith obligation and blood cost apply
  simultaneously).
- **Conditional hybrid**, where the resource behaves like one archetype
  under some conditions and shifts to another under different ones,
  creating a resource whose texture itself changes situationally.

## What it can look like on the page

- A character's capability visibly weaker or stronger depending on
  location or condition, with that variation mattering to a scene's
  tactical or plot logic.
- A hybrid resource's dual cost shown in a single scene — a character
  paying both components of their combined resource, with the tension of
  managing two obligations simultaneously made concrete.
- The inverse, as a warning sign: environmental power that never actually
  varies with the stated condition, or a hybrid resource that behaves
  identically to a single generic archetype with no trace of its second
  component.

## Progression, failure behavior, and cross-mechanic interaction

Environmental power interacts directly with plot and travel logistics,
since a character's need to stay near or reach their power source can
become a planning constraint the story engages (or a vulnerability an
antagonist can exploit by controlling access to it). Hybrid resources
interact with the resource-lifecycle-design record's full framework twice
over, since each component archetype may have its own source, access, and
limit logic that need reconciling when combined. Both interact with the
systemic-social-consequences pattern: control over a location tied to
power generation implies political and economic stakes, and a hybrid
resource's dual obligations can imply institutional structures (a faith
tradition that also demands blood sacrifice, for instance) worth at least
acknowledging.

## Common failure modes

- **Stated environmental dependency that never actually varies**, where a
  character's power is said to depend on a location or condition but
  behaves identically regardless of where or when a scene takes place.
- **A hybrid resource that's really just one archetype**, where the second
  component is named but contributes no distinct cost, behavior, or
  narrative texture.
- **Unexploited vulnerability**, where an environmentally-bound power's
  obvious strategic weakness (control of the source location) is never
  engaged by antagonists or plot despite being an evident opportunity.

## Questions for the author

- Does this character's power actually vary with the stated environmental
  condition, or does it behave identically regardless of location or
  circumstance?
- If power is location-bound, has any antagonist or plot thread engaged
  the obvious vulnerability of controlling or denying access to that
  location?
- For a hybrid resource, does each component archetype contribute a
  distinct, felt cost, or does one component do all the actual work while
  the other is decorative?
- Is there a scene where a hybrid resource's two components pull in
  different directions, creating a real tension for the character to
  manage?

## Revision or design experiments

1. For a stated environmental dependency, find or add a scene where the
   character's capability is shown varying with location or condition in a
   way that affects the outcome.
2. Introduce a plot thread where an antagonist or rival specifically
   targets a character's environmental power source.
3. For a hybrid resource, audit both components for distinct contribution;
   strengthen or cut whichever one is currently doing no narrative work.
4. Design a scene specifically to put a hybrid resource's two components in
   tension with each other.

## When this advice does not apply

- Very lightweight or narrative-only power systems don't need explicit
  environmental or hybrid mechanics developed.
- A story using environmental or hybrid framing purely as flavor, with no
  intent for it to be mechanically load-bearing, may reasonably keep the
  variation implicit rather than dramatized.

## Evidence and detection limits

Where a project explicitly tracks a resource's location- or condition-
dependent value, whether it actually varies as stated is close to
deterministic. Whether that variation, or a hybrid resource's dual cost, is
narratively engaged requires interpreting the prose and remains
model-assisted.

## Original micro-examples

*Unexploited environmental dependency:* A sorcerer is established as
drawing power from a specific grove, and the story never has any
antagonist attempt to deny, destroy, or control access to it, despite the
obvious strategic opportunity.

*Engaged dependency, tense hybrid:* The same sorcerer's power instead
combines the grove's proximity with an oath of protection to its
inhabitants (a location-bound and faith-adjacent hybrid); when an
antagonist threatens the grove's people specifically to force the
sorcerer into an impossible choice between fighting from strength (staying
to protect them, staying in range) or retreating to preserve the grove
itself, both components of the hybrid resource are in genuine, dramatized
tension.
