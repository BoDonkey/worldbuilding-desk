## What it is

Readers who specifically seek out progression fiction — whether numeric
LitRPG or narrative progression fantasy — want visible, trackable growth
as a primary source of satisfaction, not merely as a plot ingredient
serving some other story. This distinguishes the genre's core readership
from readers of, say, epic fantasy generally, where a character's growth
might be present but isn't the central, load-bearing appeal the way it is
here. A well-crafted story that happens to feature power growth
incidentally to a differently-centered plot can succeed as a novel while
still disappointing readers who came specifically for the progression
genre's particular promise — this is a genre-calibration issue distinct
from general craft quality.

## Why readers may care

This genre's readers track growth actively, often keeping mental (or
literal) tallies of a character's advancement, comparing builds, and
anticipating the next threshold. A story that treats growth as
backgrounded — happening, but not shown, tracked, or made satisfying in
its own right — asks this specific readership to accept a promise-
substitution: get an interesting plot instead of the growth-tracking
pleasure they came for. That can work if handled deliberately and
signaled early, but treated as an afterthought, it tends to read as a
missed opportunity specific to this genre's readership rather than a
general craft failure.

## Common forms and variants

- **Growth-as-primary-pleasure**, where progression is dramatized in
  detail and given significant page time as a satisfaction in its own
  right, the genre's default and most reliable form.
- **Growth-in-service-of-plot**, where progression exists and matters but
  is deliberately kept lighter-touch in favor of a differently-centered
  story — workable, but benefits from clear early signaling so the right
  readers self-select in.
- **Growth-as-mystery**, where the specific mechanics of advancement are
  deliberately obscured or gradually revealed as their own source of
  interest, satisfying progression readers' tracking instinct through
  discovery rather than transparency.
- **Multi-character growth tracking**, where an ensemble cast's
  progression is tracked comparatively, appealing to readers who enjoy
  cross-character comparison as much as single-protagonist advancement.

## What it can look like on the page

- Advancement events given dedicated narrative attention and space
  proportional to genre readers' expectation that growth is a primary
  draw, not an aside.
- A clear early signal (in tone, structure, or explicit framing) when a
  story is deliberately keeping progression lighter-touch in service of a
  different central focus.
- The inverse, as a warning sign: infrequent, minimally dramatized
  advancement events in a story otherwise signaling itself as core
  progression fantasy or LitRPG, without an apparent reason for the
  mismatch.

## Common failure modes

- **Progression treated as afterthought in a story signaled as
  progression-central**, disappointing the specific readership the
  signaling attracted.
- **No early signal when progression is deliberately backgrounded**,
  leaving readers to discover the mismatch mid-read rather than being able
  to self-select accurately from the outset.
- **Inconsistent growth-tracking density**, where advancement receives
  heavy attention early and then fades from narrative focus with no
  acknowledged shift in the story's priorities.

## Questions for the author

- Does this manuscript's advancement receive narrative attention
  proportional to what its genre signaling (title, opening chapters,
  cover-adjacent framing) has promised?
- If progression is deliberately backgrounded in favor of a different
  focus, is that choice signaled early enough for readers to accurately
  self-select?
- Has the density of dramatized advancement events changed noticeably
  across the manuscript, and if so, is that shift acknowledged or
  unexplained?

## Revision or design experiments

1. Compare the manuscript's opening signals (tone, structure, explicit
   framing) against its actual advancement-event density; adjust
   whichever is out of alignment with the other.
2. If progression is deliberately secondary to another focus, add an early,
   clear signal (in tone or structure) that lets progression-primary
   readers self-select accurately.
3. Chart advancement-event density across the manuscript and investigate
   any unexplained drop.

## When this advice does not apply

- Stories not signaling themselves as progression fantasy or LitRPG at all
  (general fantasy with incidental power growth) aren't subject to this
  genre's specific reader expectation.
- A story deliberately subverting progression-genre expectations as its
  actual point (a "progression fantasy" that reveals itself to be about
  something else entirely) is using the mismatch intentionally, provided
  it's a legible authorial choice rather than an unexamined drift.

## When straightforward execution is the right choice

A classical progression-fiction structure with growth as a consistently
dramatized, central pleasure throughout doesn't need subversion or
downplaying to succeed — delivering cleanly on this specific genre promise
is exactly what its core readership is looking for.

## Evidence and detection limits

Where a project explicitly tracks advancement events linked to scenes,
their frequency and narrative space is directly observable. Whether that
density matches the story's genre signaling, and whether readers would
experience the balance as satisfying, requires interpreting the manuscript
and genre context and remains model-assisted.

## Original micro-examples

*Mismatched promise:* A story titled and opened in classic LitRPG
conventions — a stat screen shown in chapter one — features almost no
further advancement tracking for the next thirty chapters, which instead
focus entirely on political intrigue with power growth treated as
incidental.

*Signaled and consistent:* The same political-intrigue-focused story
instead opens by establishing its actual balance explicitly — a slower,
narrative-only progression system introduced without heavy stat-tracking
conventions — so readers calibrate their expectations correctly from the
outset rather than being promised one experience and given another.
