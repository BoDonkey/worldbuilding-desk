## What it is

This record addresses mystery as a genre with distinct sub-conventions
carrying their own reader promises, complementing this library's separate
mystery-plot record, which addresses the structural fair-play mechanics
that apply across all mystery variants. A cozy mystery promises a
relatively gentle relationship to violence (death often happens off-page
or is treated lightly), a community-embedded amateur detective, and a
restoration of order at the end. Noir promises moral ambiguity, a
compromised or world-weary detective, and often an ending that doesn't
fully restore order. A procedural promises institutional competence and
process — the mechanics of investigation itself as much of the draw as
the eventual answer. These aren't the only sub-conventions, but they
illustrate how "mystery" as a label covers genuinely different reader
promises that a story needs to calibrate to and signal clearly.

## Why readers may care

A reader who picks up a story signaled as cozy and encounters noir's moral
compromise and unresolved order (or vice versa) experiences a specific
kind of genre-promise violation distinct from an unfair mystery reveal —
the puzzle can be perfectly fair-played and the sub-genre mismatch will
still frustrate readers who came for a specific relationship to violence,
morality, and resolution that the story isn't delivering.

## Common forms and variants

- **Cozy mystery**, gentle violence, community setting, amateur detective,
  restored order — promises comfort alongside puzzle-solving.
- **Noir**, moral ambiguity, a compromised detective, often unresolved or
  bittersweet order — promises atmosphere and complexity alongside puzzle-
  solving.
- **Procedural**, institutional process and competence as central
  interest, often with a professional detective operating within a
  system — promises methodical engagement alongside puzzle-solving.
- **Hardboiled**, closely related to noir but often more action-forward
  and terse in style — promises a specific voice and pace alongside
  puzzle-solving.
- **Genre-hybrid mystery**, combining mystery's puzzle structure with
  another genre's conventions (a fantasy mystery, a LitRPG mystery),
  requiring both genres' promises to be calibrated together rather than
  one undercutting the other.

## What it can look like on the page

- A story's early tone, violence level, and detective characterization
  consistent with a specific, legible sub-convention throughout.
- A resolution whose degree of restored order (full, partial, or absent)
  matches what the story's sub-convention promised from the start.
- The inverse, as a warning sign: a story opening with cozy signals
  (light tone, community warmth, gentle stakes) that shifts into
  noir-level moral compromise and unresolved consequence with no
  signaled transition.

## Common failure modes

- **Sub-convention drift**, where a story begins signaling one mystery
  sub-genre and ends delivering a different one, without the shift being
  a deliberate, signaled choice.
- **A hybrid mystery where the other genre's conventions overwhelm the
  puzzle**, leaving readers who came for the mystery half feeling
  shortchanged.
- **Mismatched detective characterization**, where a detective's
  established competence, morality, or worldview doesn't match the
  sub-genre's usual expectations, creating a subtle but persistent
  friction.

## Questions for the author

- Which mystery sub-convention (cozy, noir, procedural, or another) does
  this story's opening actually signal, and does the rest of the
  manuscript deliver consistently on that signal?
- Does the ending's degree of restored order match what the opening
  promised?
- If this is a genre hybrid, do both genres' conventions get calibrated
  together, or does one undercut the other?
- Is the detective's characterization (competence, moral outlook)
  consistent with the sub-convention the story is otherwise signaling?

## Revision or design experiments

1. Identify which mystery sub-convention the opening chapters signal, and
   audit the rest of the manuscript for consistency with that signal.
2. For a story that drifts between sub-conventions, either adjust the
   opening to signal the eventual tone, or add a legible transition point.
3. For a genre hybrid, check that neither genre's conventions are
   silently overwhelming the other.

## When this advice does not apply

- A story deliberately subverting its own established sub-convention (a
  cozy mystery that reveals genuine, unresolved darkness beneath its
  comfortable surface, as its actual subject) is using the shift
  intentionally, provided it's legible as a choice.
- Very light or brief mystery elements within a larger, non-mystery story
  don't need a fully calibrated sub-convention.

## When straightforward execution is the right choice

A classical, consistently executed cozy, noir, or procedural mystery,
without genre-blending or subversion, remains a satisfying and reliable
structure — genre readers specifically seeking one of these established
promises are well served by a story that delivers it cleanly.

## Evidence and detection limits

Whether a story's tone, violence level, and resolution consistently match
a specific mystery sub-convention requires reading and interpreting the
manuscript's overall register — a judgment no structured data substitutes
for.

## Original micro-examples

*Sub-convention drift:* A mystery opens in a small, warm village with a
gently curious amateur detective and light stakes, then shifts by the
midpoint into unresolved moral compromise and permanent, unmourned loss,
with no signal in the opening preparing readers for the tonal shift.

*Consistent sub-convention:* The same opening instead is followed by a
resolution that, while solving the central puzzle, restores the village's
sense of order and safety — consistent with the cozy promise the opening
made, even as the mystery itself involves real stakes along the way.
