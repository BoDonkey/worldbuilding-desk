## What it is

GameLit is the widest umbrella subgenre: game-like worlds with quests,
respawns, and NPCs, where mechanics are thematic rather than numerical and
there's no obligation to show a stat screen at all. LitRPG sits inside this
umbrella as the subset that does require explicit, visible mechanics.

## Why readers may care

The promise is game-world fun without parsing stat tables — readers who want
the flavor and structure of a game-like world without LitRPG's numeric
density are specifically served by this subgenre, and applying LitRPG
conventions to a GameLit manuscript risks serving a promise the story never
made.

## Common forms and variants

GameLit ranges from stories with almost no game-mechanical vocabulary at all
(quests and NPCs as narrative framing devices) to stories that gesture at
mechanics thematically without ever displaying numbers. The boundary with
classic LitRPG is the single clearest dividing line in the whole taxonomy:
numeric versus narrative progression.

## What this profile modifies in other records

- **Stat-block density**, **system-as-narrator intrusion**, and
  **meaningless numbers** (referenced pattern) largely do not apply — a book
  with no stat blocks has no stat-block density problem, and a book with no
  system voice has nothing to intrude. This is the subgenre where a coach
  assuming LitRPG conventions will be most obviously and immediately wrong.
- **Hard numbers versus named tiers** resolves toward neither pole in the
  usual sense — GameLit frequently uses neither precise numbers nor formal
  tier names, conveying growth entirely through action and consequence.
- **Visible versus hidden systems** may not apply either, if there is no
  formal "system" to make visible or hidden in the first place.

## What it can look like on the page

- Quests, NPCs, and game-adjacent structure conveyed through narrative
  action, with no displayed numeric or formally tiered progression.
- Game vocabulary used loosely and thematically (a character "grinding" a
  skill through practice, narrated without any stat readout).
- The inverse, as a signal this profile doesn't fit: displayed stat sheets
  or explicit level-up notifications — likely better matched to the
  classic-LitRPG profile instead.

## Characteristic failure mode

Neither fish nor fowl — too gamey for general fantasy readers, too soft for
LitRPG readers who want visible mechanics, satisfying neither promise fully.
This is a tonal and expectation-management risk more than a structural
craft failure.

## Revision or design experiments

1. Confirm early in the manuscript whether numeric mechanics will ever be
   displayed; if not, make that clear to the reader quickly so LitRPG-
   trained expectations don't set in and then go unmet.
2. If the manuscript sits uneasily between GameLit and LitRPG, consider
   which promise is primary and lean the opening chapters toward
   establishing that promise clearly.
3. Audit any coaching notes generated for this manuscript and discard any
   that assume a stat screen or system voice that doesn't exist in the text.

## Questions for the author

- Does this manuscript ever display explicit numeric mechanics, or is all
  progression conveyed narratively?
- If a reader arrived expecting classic LitRPG's numeric density, would the
  opening chapters correct that expectation quickly enough?
- Is game vocabulary used consistently as theme/flavor, or does it
  intermittently promise mechanical specificity the story doesn't deliver?

## When this profile does not apply

A manuscript with any meaningful displayed numeric mechanics should be
evaluated primarily under the classic-LitRPG or progression-fantasy profile
instead; treating a numerically explicit manuscript as GameLit would
suppress genuinely applicable stat-presentation coaching.

## Evidence and detection limits

Whether a manuscript displays numeric mechanics is directly observable from
the text. Whether the resulting tonal positioning (game-adjacent but not
numeric) serves the story's intended readership requires interpreting genre
expectations and remains model-assisted.

## Original micro-examples

*Numeric drift into unclear territory:* A GameLit-framed story occasionally
mentions a character's "level" in passing dialogue without ever displaying
a number or a formal tier name, creating ambiguity about whether the system
is meant to be tracked or purely atmospheric.

*Clear GameLit framing:* The same story instead consistently narrates growth
through demonstrated capability only ("she could finally hold the heavier
blade steady") with no numeric or tier vocabulary at all, giving the reader
a clear, consistent signal about what kind of progression story this is.
