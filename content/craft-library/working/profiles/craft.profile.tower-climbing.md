## What it is

Tower climbing structures progression vertically through self-contained
floors, each with its own ecosystem, rules, and escalating tier, usually
built around an overarching mystery about the tower itself. The structure
gives the subgenre what genre research calls the cleanest progression hook
available: the next floor is always the next legible goal.

## Why readers may care

The promise is clean, legible progression with a built-in sense of forward
motion — a reader always knows what "success" looks like next (reach the
next floor), which lowers the barrier to sustained engagement across a long
serialized run. The risk that comes with that legibility is repetition: a
structure this clean can default into "new floor, same beat" if each floor
doesn't do distinct narrative work.

## Common forms and variants

Some tower structures are physically vertical and literal; others use
"floor" as a metaphor for any self-contained, gated stage (trial rooms,
dungeon levels, ranked leagues). The tower's own nature — natural, built,
or sentient — and the mystery around it varies widely and is often a
significant plot thread in its own right.

## What this profile modifies in other records

- **The decorative chapter test** is strongest here and should probably be
  phrased per-floor rather than strictly per-chapter: what does this floor
  ask that the last one did not? This is the best-suited subgenre in the
  corpus for that reframing.
- **Advancement rate** maps cleanly onto floor-by-floor progression,
  making floor-completion interval an unusually clean, near-deterministic
  metric where floor completion is explicitly tracked.
- Structurally, this is flagged in the source research as the best proving
  ground for any dashboard-style structural pass, since floor boundaries are
  unusually clean units of analysis.

## What it can look like on the page

- Each floor introducing a genuinely new ecosystem, rule, or type of
  problem, distinct from the floor before it.
- A running mystery about the tower's nature or purpose, developed
  incrementally alongside floor-by-floor progression.
- The inverse, as a warning sign: floors distinguished only by a reskinned
  setting (a new biome) with the same beat — arrive, fight, advance —
  repeated with no structural variation.

## Characteristic failure mode

Floors become interchangeable — new biome, same beat — so structure
substitutes for stakes, and the clean legibility that makes tower climbing
appealing curdles into predictability.

## Revision or design experiments

1. For each floor in a stretch, name what specifically is new about it
   (not just its setting, but its rules, threat type, or moral complexity);
   flag any floor where the answer is only cosmetic.
2. Apply the decorative chapter test per floor rather than per chapter for
   one arc, and compare the results to a per-chapter application.
3. Check whether the tower's central mystery advances at a pace that keeps
   readers engaged across the floor-by-floor structure, rather than
   remaining static for long stretches.

## Questions for the author

- What does this floor ask of the protagonist that the last floor didn't?
- Is the tower's mystery advancing at a pace proportional to how many floors
  have passed?
- If floor order were shuffled, would anything about the protagonist's arc
  or the reader's understanding change?

## When this profile does not apply

A vertically structured story without discrete, self-contained
"floor"-style stages (a continuous, unstaged journey) should use a general
progression-fantasy or quest framing instead; applying per-floor
decorative-chapter-test phrasing to a manuscript with no floor structure
would misapply a scope this profile assumes.

## Evidence and detection limits

Whether a manuscript is structured around discrete floor-like stages is
directly observable from its structure. Whether each floor does distinct
narrative work, or merely reskins the last one, requires interpreting the
prose and remains model-assisted; where floor completion is explicitly
tracked as structured data, interval metrics are close to deterministic.

## Original micro-examples

*Interchangeable floors:* Floor twelve is a desert with sand-monsters;
floor thirteen is a forest with plant-monsters; both are resolved with the
same tactic (find the chokepoint, fight through), and neither raises a new
kind of problem.

*Distinct floors:* Floor thirteen instead introduces a rule where combat is
forbidden and progress requires negotiation with the floor's inhabitants — a
genuinely new kind of problem the protagonist's usual combat-focused
toolkit can't solve.
