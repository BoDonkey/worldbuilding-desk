## What it is

Base building applies progression logic to a growing community rather than
(or in addition to) an individual protagonist: settlement upgrades, NPC
recruitment with roles and loyalty, resource logistics, and siege events
structure the story's escalation.

## Why readers may care

The promise is macro satisfaction — watching a small, vulnerable start
(a campfire, a single shelter) grow into a thriving settlement or kingdom —
paired with the more intimate satisfaction of individual recruits mattering
as the community grows. The subgenre's central risk is management becoming
an end in itself, disconnected from any character the reader is invested in.

## Common forms and variants

Some base-building stories keep a single protagonist as the settlement's
clear leader and viewpoint; others distribute viewpoint across several
settlement members, closer to a true ensemble structure. Overlap with
dungeon core is common, since both concern managing a growing structure,
though base-building's protagonist is typically mobile and socially
embedded rather than the structure itself.

## What this profile modifies in other records

- **Found family** applies at particular strength — the recruited cast is
  often the subject of the story, not a supporting structure around a
  single protagonist's arc.
- **Power as sole motivation** needs a variant: the protagonist's goal is
  legitimately communal (the settlement's survival and flourishing) rather
  than personal, and evaluating individual want/need should be applied to
  cast members rather than assumed to require a single unifying personal
  goal.
- **Side-cast obsolescence** (referenced pattern pool concept) fully
  inverts here: the supporting cast is the subject, not a liability, and a
  coach warning about side-cast irrelevance in this subgenre has likely
  misread the book.
- **Vertical versus horizontal progression** applies at the settlement
  level as well as the individual level — new buildings, roles, and
  specializations function as horizontal growth for the community as a
  whole.

## What it can look like on the page

- Named settlement members with individual roles, loyalty, and stakes that
  develop over time, rather than an undifferentiated population.
- Resource and logistics decisions that carry visible consequences for
  specific characters, not just aggregate settlement statistics.
- The inverse, as a warning sign: settlement growth tracked purely through
  aggregate numbers (population count, building tiers) with no individual
  character affected by or invested in that growth.

## Characteristic failure mode

Management without personal stakes — the settlement grows and no one in it
matters, so escalating aggregate numbers substitute for the character
investment that would make the growth feel meaningful.

## Revision or design experiments

1. Name three settlement members with individual stakes in the community's
   growth, and find a scene for each where their specific stake surfaces.
2. Audit a stretch of settlement-management content and check whether
   growth is shown affecting a specific character, or only reported as
   aggregate statistics.
3. For a siege or crisis event, ground the stakes in what it would cost a
   specific named character, not only the settlement in aggregate.

## Questions for the author

- Which specific settlement members does the reader have reason to care
  about individually, distinct from the community as a whole?
- Does settlement growth ever get tracked purely through aggregate numbers
  with no character-level consequence attached?
- If the protagonist's goal is communal rather than personal, is that
  communal goal given the same dramatized weight a personal want would
  receive?

## When this profile does not apply

A story with a single mobile protagonist and no recruited, developing
community should use progression-fantasy or another individual-focused
profile instead; applying the side-cast-obsolescence inversion to a
story without a genuine ensemble cast would misapply a modifier meant for
communal structures specifically.

## Evidence and detection limits

Whether a manuscript centers community growth and a recruited cast is
directly observable from its structure. Whether that growth carries
individual character stakes, or remains purely aggregate, requires
interpreting the prose and remains model-assisted.

## Original micro-examples

*Aggregate-only growth:* A settlement's population grows from twelve to two
hundred across a manuscript, reported through periodic status updates, with
no named resident's personal stake in that growth ever dramatized.

*Character-grounded growth:* The same growth instead includes a specific
former refugee who arrived starving in chapter two and is shown, by chapter
thirty, training the settlement's new arrivals in the same skill that once
saved her — the aggregate number and a specific character's arc reinforce
each other.
